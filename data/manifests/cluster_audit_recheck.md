## Q4. Is CLN3 <-> NPC supported as shared mechanism by the paper's own wording?
Partly. The authors do use unhedged shared-mechanism wording in the abstract Interpretation: "Our findings also support that JNCL and NPC disease share pathogenic pathways leading to aberrant lysosomal accumulation of lipids and proteins". But the surrounding hedges are material:
- same sentence: "and thus suggest that the treatments available for NPC disease may be beneficial to JNCL patients";
- Discussion final paragraph: "JNCL and NPC may share some pathogenic pathways";
- Findings: "Lipid profiles ... were similar ... except for levels of ... (BMP)" and "Protein profiles ... appeared identical, except for levels of NPC1" (so NPC1 and BMP differ);
- Discussion: "we were not able to make a solid conclusion regarding mannose-6-phosphate containing proteins in human brain samples in JNCL patients";
- the paper's own premise was a hypothesis ("we hypothesised that dysfunction of CLN3 leads to the aberrant accumulation of cholesterol").
The audit quotes the strong sentence in C05 and lists the "may be beneficial" hedge in the limits column, so it is not misquoting, but "shares_pathogenic_pathway" as a graph predicate should carry "may share some" and the N=5 vs 3-4 caveat. Evidence is correlative, one lab, one brain region (BA9), post-mortem, NPC cases younger (mean 16.8 vs ~24 y).

## Verdict: PARTLY SUPPORTED
CLN3 + NPC is the best-documented pair in the audit: a primary human-tissue paper (6/5/3 lipidomics) states cholesterol storage comparable to NPC and shared pathways, with consistent supporting data on the CLN3 side (C07, C08) and for NPC (C09). It is only partly supported because: the shared-pathway claim is hedged by the authors ("may share some pathogenic pathways"), N is very small, BMP and NPC1 differ, and the "opposing direction" evidence for NPC human tissue (C10) does not hold up as cited. Registry assets are verified for NPC (INPDR) and NCL (DEM-CHILD), but no registry spans both and access terms are unknown; the CLN7 natural-history arm and the CLN3/CLN6 study are discontinued/terminated. Would move to SUPPORTED: expert confirmation that the cohort and BMP interpretation are sound, independent replication, and access terms for the registries. Would move to NOT SUPPORTED: expert judgment that the 2023 cohort cannot carry a shared-mechanism edge, or a retraction/correction (none found).
